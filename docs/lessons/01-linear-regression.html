Lesson 1: Predicting Birth Weight - Linear Regression

Introduction to the Domain

Birth weight is a key indicator of newborn health; low birth weight is linked to infant mortality and developmental problems. Maternal factors (age, weight, smoking, hypertension, etc.) are associated with birth weight. Linear regression gives an interpretable baseline: each coefficient estimates the change in expected birth weight per unit change in a predictor.

Research background

Predicting birth weight from maternal characteristics is a classic problem in perinatal epidemiology. Kramer (1987) synthesized decades of work showing that maternal nutrition, smoking, and hypertension are among the strongest determinants of low birth weight. Wilcox (2001) reminds us that birth weight is a proxy, not a cause—models should be interpreted carefully and not over-interpreted as causal. For an accessible overview, see Wilcox, On the importance—and the unimportance—of birthweight, International Journal of Epidemiology (2001).

Citations: - Kramer MS. Determinants of low birth weight: methodological assessment and meta-analysis. Bulletin of the World Health Organization (1987). - Wilcox AJ. On the importance—and the unimportance—of birthweight. International Journal of Epidemiology (2001).

Research Question

Which maternal factors predict infant birth weight, and how do we interpret the coefficients and model fit?

Open-Source Dataset

Low Birth Weight (birthwt) dataset (MASS / Kaggle).

  • Link: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/abhishek14398/low-birth-weight (or MASS::birthwt in R)
  • Size: ~189 rows.
  • Outcome: bwt (birth weight in grams).
  • Predictors: mother’s age, weight, race, smoking, hypertension, uterine irritability, physician visits, etc.
  • Notes: small sample; check for outliers and skew.

Practical Exercise

Model to explore

Simple and multiple linear regression (statsmodels OLS / scikit-learn LinearRegression). Focus on interpretation, not just prediction.

AI prompts to learn this model

  • “Explain simple linear regression to an undergraduate: what is it minimizing, and what does the slope mean?”
  • “What are the four assumptions of OLS, and how do I check each one with plots?”

Starter code

import pandas as pd
import statsmodels.api as sm

df = pd.read_csv("birthwt.csv")
# THINK: why drop only the outcome (and ID columns) and not other columns?
X = df.drop(columns=["bwt", "id"])
y = df["bwt"]
# THINK: why add a constant? What happens if you forget it?
X = sm.add_constant(X)
# THINK: why use statsmodels OLS here instead of sklearn LinearRegression?
model = sm.OLS(  # THINK: what assumptions does OLS require?y, X).fit()
print(model.summary())

Lines to wrestle with (THINK)

  • X = df.drop(...) — which columns are legitimate predictors vs. leakage/ID?
  • sm.add_constant(X) — what is the intercept doing, and how does its meaning change with standardization?
  • sm.OLS( # THINK: what assumptions does OLS require?y, X) — OLS assumes linearity, independence, homoscedasticity, normality of residuals. Which of these can you check from model.summary()?

Suggested AI prompts

  • “Explain the coefficient for smoke in plain language, including units.”
  • “What does R-squared vs. adjusted R-squared tell me here?”
  • “Which OLS assumptions are most likely violated in this data, and how would I check?”

Extensions

  • Standardize predictors and re-fit; how do coefficients change?
  • Add an interaction (age x smoking) and test significance.
  • Plot residuals vs. fitted to check homoscedasticity.

Deliverables

  1. A table of coefficients with 95% confidence intervals and p-values (from model.summary() or model.conf_int()).
  2. A residual plot (residuals vs. fitted) and a Q-Q plot of residuals.
  3. Written answers (3-5 sentences each):
    • What does the coefficient of the strongest predictor mean in clinical terms?
    • State the four OLS assumptions and which is most concerning for this dataset.
    • Why might a linear model be a poor choice for predicting birth weight, and what would you try next?
    • How would you guard against overfitting with only ~189 rows?
